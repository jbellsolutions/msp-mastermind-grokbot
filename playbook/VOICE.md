# How the messages sound

The readers run managed service providers. They are technical, they get pitched all day, and they can spot an automated message in one line.

- Write as one MSP owner to another. Plain words, short sentences, under 90 words. One idea, one easy question.
- Open with the true reason for this person: where the two met, what they built, the specialty the room lacks. If there is no true reason, the person is not ready for a message.
- The ask is a conversation. No price, no link and no attachment in a first message unless the person asked.
- Say what it is only when it is natural: a small group of MSP owners helping each other.
- No hype, no urgency, no "quick question", no "hope this finds you well", no flattery, no invented facts about their company.
- Seats are stated only as they truly are.
- Use the host's own phrases from owner.md and from messages the owner sent before, when there are any in data/contacts/voice/.
- A no gets a thank you and nothing after it.

## Shapes that start conversations

**Met before.** Where you met, one thing they said or do, "would you be up for a short call to hear what you're building?"

**Same network.** The network you are both in, why you noticed them, "I'm getting to know other owners who do <specialty>. Open to comparing notes?"

**A peer found in public.** The specific thing they published or do, what the host sees in their own shop, "would a 15 minute call be useful to both of us?"

**Second and third note.** Two lines. Something useful or a real question. Never "just following up". Three messages in all, then later.
